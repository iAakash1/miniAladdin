# EXP-005 — Does any additional data source add information over price?

**FINDING: NO. Every additional source tested makes the model worse.**

Options, analyst estimate revisions and announcement-gated statement
fundamentals were each added to a price-volatility-volume-macro base. None
improved it. The best result in the entire study comes from the arm containing
**no fundamental data at all**, and it still fails every economic gate.

The registry holds zero production models. The holdout remains locked.

---

## 1. The question, and why it was worth asking

EXP-004 found no edge in the feature set it had. Two readings survive that: the
signal is not there, or the feature set never contained it. Almost all the data
in this repository was unused — 116M option-chain rows, 14.1M analyst estimate
vintages and five statement tables were catalogued and idle — so the second
reading was live.

EXP-005 settles it by adding one source at a time and measuring the contrast.

## 2. What was newly wired in

| Family | Source | Rows | Point-in-time basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `estimates` | `eps_estimate`, `sales_estimate` | 7,060,412 each | **Observation-dated.** No gate needed |
| `fundamentals` | income, balance ×3, cash flow | ~1.3M | **Period-keyed.** Announcement gate required |

### The gate, quantified

`income_statement.date` is a fiscal **period end**, not a filing date. Verified
on AAPL: the quarter ending 2026-06-30 was announced 2026-07-30; the quarter
ending 2025-12-31 was announced 2026-01-29. 270,888 of 270,925 rows fall on a
month end.

Every period is therefore joined **forward** to its first `earnings_calendar`
announcement. **119,602 periods matched; 77,277 were dropped** for want of an
announcement — almost all pre-2020, where the calendar has no coverage. Nothing
is estimated from a conventional 45- or 90-day lag.

### The trap in the estimate panel

`period` is *relative* ('Current Year'), so at a fiscal rollover the label starts
describing a different year and consensus jumps. Differencing across that
boundary manufactures a huge revision on a predictable calendar — exactly the
spurious regularity a tree model will fit. Revisions are computed **only where
`period_end_date` is unchanged** between vintages. This costs coverage: the
13-week revision populates 50% of rows against the 4-week's 82%.

### What the options schema cannot support

Not implemented, because the columns do not exist: put/call **volume**
imbalance, **open-interest** imbalance, dealer **gamma exposure**, **unusual
activity**. `option_chain` carries bid, ask, IV and Greeks — no volume, no open
interest. Claiming those signals would mean inventing them.

* **Options — 8 GB, 116.5M rows — do not help.** 1 of 6 models improved.
* **Analyst revisions do not help.** 2 of 6, and the mean is negative. This was

  — and the most defensible point-in-time basis of anything added.
* **Statement fundamentals actively hurt**, by the largest margin of any source:
  −0.0210 mean, 0 of 6 models improved.
* **Everything together is worse than the base.**

## 3.1 The t = +2.66, and why it is not a finding

This is the number in this study most likely to be misread, including by me, so
it gets its own section and the arithmetic is shown.

**It is a maximum over 42 configurations**, selected after the fact. The relevant
question is not "is 2.66 significant for one test" but "what does the best of 42
— or of 139 — zero-skill configurations look like?"

| | |
|---|---|
| Observed max \|t\| across the ablation | **+2.658** |
| Bonferroni threshold, 42 arm tests | \|t\| > **3.24** |
| Bonferroni threshold, 139 cumulative trials | \|t\| > **3.57** |
| **E[max \|t\|] under the null, 139 zero-skill configs** | **≈ 2.62** (95th pct 3.36) |

**The best t-statistic in the entire study is +2.66. Pure noise across 139 trials
would be expected to produce +2.62.** The observed maximum is almost exactly the
null expectation. It clears no correction at either count.

**And it is unstable.** The same model, same folds, same seed, only the feature
columns changing:

| Arm | `gradient_boosting` t |
|---|---|
| `A_price` | +1.21 |
| `B_price_vol` | +1.38 |
| **`C_base`** | **+2.66** |
| `D_base_options` | +1.74 |
| `E_base_estimates` | +2.16 |
| `F_base_fundamentals` | +0.24 |
| `G_all` | +0.35 |

A spread of **2.42** in the t-statistic from feature-set composition alone. A
robust effect does not do that. This is what selecting a maximum out of a noisy
surface looks like from the inside.

**No economics were computed for the arms.** The ablation measures rank
information only — no backtest, no execution lag, no costs, no turnover. On the
full feature set every learned model has a negative *gross* Sharpe, and there is
no reason to assume `C_base` differs. Until that is measured, +0.0290 is a
correlation, not a strategy.

**The correct disposition.** `C_base` — 27 features, price and volatility and
volume and the rate curve, no fundamental data at all — is now the best available
*hypothesis*. It is not a result. Testing it properly means a new pre-registered
experiment with its own declared trial budget, a full cost-aware backtest, and a
significance threshold set in advance. Spending the holdout on it now would be
spending a single-use asset on the maximum of a noisy search.

## 4. Why more data made it worse

Not a mystery, and worth stating because it generalises. Adding a family adds
columns that are mostly noise for this target; a tree model spends splits on
them, and a linear model spends coefficients. The base has 27 features and the
full set 57, so more than half the inputs in `G_all` come from sources the
ablation shows carry nothing.

The linear models make it visible. `ridge` and `elastic_net` sit at +0.005 on
`C_base` and at **−0.020** on `F_base_fundamentals` — the fundamental columns do
not merely fail to help, they pull the fit in the wrong direction. Coverage is
part of it: `fund_*` reaches only 53% of panel rows because the announcement gate
drops everything pre-2020, so the imputer is filling a large fraction of a wide
block with fold medians.

| **low-volatility bull** | **267** | **+0.0019** | **+0.17** | **yes** |

dates is the most cherry-pickable number this pipeline produces, and reporting

story about a model that "works in stressed markets."

## 9. A reporting defect in this study, and what was done about it

The first pass recorded every arm's IC and a **blank** for every arm's
t-statistic, train IC and fold rate. `_run_ablation` read the leaderboard's field
names off `pooled_ic`, where the t-statistic is stored as `t_stat` and the rest
come from `result.stability(...)`. Reading a missing key returns `None` rather
than raising, so the table rendered with four empty columns.

The fits were correct; only the transcription was wrong. `rerun_ablation.py`
recomputes the block from the same definition, dataset hash and seed, and
**refuses to merge unless every mean IC reproduces exactly** — drift would mean
something other than the reporting had changed. A regression test
(`test_ablation_reporting.py`) now pins both readers to the same field names.

The trial count does not move. Re-running a fixed pre-registered configuration to
recover a mis-transcribed metric adds no selection freedom.

## 11. Should the holdout be opened?

**No.**

A holdout confirms a candidate that development evidence has already made
plausible. After 139 cumulative evaluations there is still no such candidate:

* the only |t| > 2 belongs to a deliberately over-parameterised control;
* the best genuine learned model loses to two free baselines;
* every gross Sharpe among learned models is negative — they lose before costs;
* every alpha t is negative except a 1993 factor's, which is not significant;
* the one regime with enough data shows +0.002 at t +0.17;
* every deflated-Sharpe probability is 0.000 against 139 trials;
* and the single best t-statistic anywhere — `C_base` gradient_boosting at
  **+2.66** — is below what the best of 139 zero-skill configurations would be
  expected to produce (**≈ 2.62**), clears neither Bonferroni threshold, swings
  by 2.42 across feature sets, and has never been costed (§3.1).

`HOLDOUT_CONTRACT.md` remains **NOT ARMED**, the firewall refuses holdout rows at
fit time, and `--preflight` refuses on `contract_armed`.

## 12. What this study is worth

The negative is the headline, but it is a *specific, actionable* negative rather
than an absence of findings:

1. **Three data sources have been priced.** Options, analyst revisions and gated
   fundamentals do not add information over price, volatility, volume and the
   rate curve, in this universe at this horizon. That is a reason to stop paying
   to store and maintain 8 GB of option chains for this purpose.
2. **The best feature set is the smallest useful one.** `C_base` — 27 features,
   all derivable from the price panel and the Treasury curve — beats the
   57-feature set by a wide margin on every model, and produced the study's
   highest t-statistic. That makes it the **best available hypothesis for a
   future pre-registered experiment**, with its own trial budget and a
   cost-aware backtest. It is not a result (§3.1).
3. **The apparatus held.** Truncation invariance clean over 1.1M rows × 103
   features; both blocking controls ≈ 0; the firewall engaged throughout; a
   reporting defect caught, fixed, and pinned by a test.

**Category: NO EVIDENCE OF EDGE, and no evidence that more data supplies one.**
